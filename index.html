About Me

Linking phenotype, genetics, and molecular mechanisms under combined stress.

I am a plant scientist and Postdoctoral Research Associate in Plant Systems Biology, Biosciences Division, at Oak Ridge National Laboratory. My research investigates how trees and crops respond to combined abiotic stresses, including heat, drought, salinity, waterlogging, and interacting environmental challenges under climate change.

I use Advanced Plant Phenotyping Laboratory facilities to support comprehensive phenotyping research. My work combines RGB imaging, chlorophyll fluorescence, thermal imaging, VNIR/SWIR hyperspectral imaging, whole-plant physiology, stress-biomarker analysis, population genomics, genome-wide association studies, transcriptomics, metabolomics, and proteomics.

My research includes Populus species and crops such as soybean, wheat, barley, chilli, tomato, cotton, and sorghum. I aim to connect controlled-environment phenotyping with field validation to identify biologically meaningful and agriculturally useful resilience traits.

Research Expertise

Research areas

My research examines plant performance under multiple interacting environmental stresses and links visible phenotypic traits with physiological, genetic, and molecular mechanisms.

01

Combined Abiotic Stress Physiology

Plant responses to drought, heat, salinity, waterlogging, and their combinations, including growth, photosynthesis, water relations, oxidative stress, and recovery.

02

High-Throughput Plant Phenotyping

Non-destructive plant characterization using RGB, chlorophyll fluorescence, thermal, and hyperspectral imaging across VNIR and SWIR wavelengths.

03

Stress Biomarkers and Physiology

Gas exchange, chlorophyll fluorescence, plant water status, oxidative-stress indicators, hormone responses, ion balance, and other physiological stress biomarkers.

04

Population Genomics and GWAS

Population genomics and genome-wide association studies to connect natural genetic variation with stress-response traits and candidate genes.

05

Multi-Omics Integration

Integration of transcriptomics, metabolomics, proteomics, genomics, physiology, and phenomics to understand resilience mechanisms under stress.

06

Field Validation

Validating key traits and stress-response mechanisms under field conditions to improve agricultural relevance and climate resilience.

Scientific Insights

Questions guiding my research

Insight 01

Combined stresses are not simply additive

Plant responses to drought plus heat, salinity plus waterlogging, or other stress combinations cannot always be predicted from responses to individual stresses.

Insight 02

Stress responses are dynamic

Repeated phenotyping and physiology measurements help identify stress onset, acclimation, injury, recovery, and resilience over time.

Insight 03

Phenotyping requires biological interpretation

Imaging provides scalable trait data, while physiological and biochemical measurements help explain their biological meaning.

Insight 04

Natural variation enables discovery

Diverse tree and crop populations contain valuable variation in stress tolerance that can be explored through population genomics and GWAS.

Insight 05

Multi-omics connects phenotype to mechanism

Omics data can reveal pathways and candidate genes associated with phenotypic differences in tolerance, susceptibility, and recovery.

Insight 06

Field validation is essential

Controlled experiments provide precision, while field validation determines whether traits remain stable and relevant in real agricultural systems.
