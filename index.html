About Me

I am a plant scientist specializing in abiotic stress physiology. My research focuses on the physiological, biochemical, molecular, and hormonal mechanisms that enable plants to survive and perform under unfavorable environmental conditions.


My broader research goal is to contribute to the development of climate-smart, stress-resilient, and sustainable crop-production systems in response to climate change and global food-security challenges.

Research Interests

My research addresses important environmental constraints affecting crop productivity and explores practical biological strategies for improving stress tolerance in plants.

Drought Stress

Plant water relations, osmotic adjustment, photosynthetic performance, drought acclimation, and stress-memory mechanisms.

Salinity Stress

Salt-stress physiology, ion balance, antioxidant activity, phytohormones, and salinity-tolerant crop development.

Temperature Stress

Plant responses to heat and cold stress, including oxidative damage, photosynthetic limitations, and protective mechanisms.

Waterlogging Stress

Plant adaptation to hypoxia, root-zone stress, growth limitation, flooding, and waterlogging-resilient crop systems.

Heavy-Metal Stress

Plant defense responses, phytotoxicity, oxidative stress, and physiological strategies for stress mitigation.

Climate-Smart Crops

Crop resilience, stress-tolerance traits, biotechnology, sustainable agriculture, and future food security.
