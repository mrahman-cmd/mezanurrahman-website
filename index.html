About Me

Linking phenotype to mechanism under climate stress.

I am a plant scientist and Postdoctoral Research Associate in Plant Systems Biology at Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL). My current work uses the Advanced Plant Phenotyping Laboratory to characterise heat and drought responses in Populus trichocarpa using RGB, chlorophyll fluorescence, thermal, and hyperspectral imaging approaches.

A second strand of my work is method development: designing genome-wide association-scale experiments, optimising stress assays and imaging workflows, and integrating phenotypic data with omics layers so that results are reproducible and interpretable.

I completed my Ph.D. in Plant and Soil Science at Texas Tech University. My previous research investigated drought, heat, salinity, waterlogging, stress signalling, phytohormones, plant–microbe interactions, and crop stress resilience in soybean, cotton, and other plant systems.

Research Expertise

Research areas

Current focus at ORNL

Heat and drought physiology of Populus trichocarpa, studied through non-destructive phenotyping, whole-plant physiology, and population-scale experimental design.

01

High-Throughput Phenotyping

Image-based quantification of plant performance through RGB, chlorophyll fluorescence, thermal, and VNIR/SWIR hyperspectral imaging to track stress onset, severity, and recovery.

02

Heat and Drought Physiology

Whole-plant and leaf-level responses of Populus trichocarpa to heat, drought, and their combination, with emphasis on genotypic variation in tolerance and recovery.

03

Experimental Design for Genetic Mapping

Planning replicated GWAS-scale stress experiments across numerous genotypes, including batching, randomisation, controls, and data-quality procedures.

04

Phenotyping Method Optimisation

Developing and benchmarking stress assays and imaging workflows, including the effects of assay duration, imaging modality, trait extraction, and scoring approach.

05

Multi-Omics Integration

Connecting phenotypic variation with transcriptomic, metabolomic, physiological, and genomic information to identify mechanisms of stress tolerance.

06

Photosynthetic Resilience

Gas exchange, chlorophyll fluorescence, and oxidative stress measurements as functional readouts that connect imaging traits to plant physiology.

Earlier and continuing work

Crop stress physiology and signalling, plant–microbe interactions, abiotic stress memory, sustainable agriculture, agroforestry, and tree–crop interaction research.
