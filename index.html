How do trees and crops withstand combined abiotic stresses?

Oak Ridge National Laboratory · Advanced Plant Phenotyping Laboratory · Tennessee, USA

I investigate how trees and crops respond to interacting abiotic stresses, including heat, drought, salinity, waterlogging, and their combinations. By integrating high-throughput imaging, whole-plant physiology, stress-biomarker analysis, population genomics (PopGen), genome-wide association studies (GWAS), and multi-omics—including transcriptomics, metabolomics, and proteomics—I identify stress-response traits and mechanisms that support climate resilience. My research includes Populus species and crop systems such as soybean, wheat, barley, chilli, and tomato. Findings from controlled-environment studies are further validated under field conditions to support biologically meaningful and agriculturally relevant outcomes.

About Me

Connecting phenotype, physiology, genetics, and molecular mechanisms.

I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate in Plant Systems Biology at Oak Ridge National Laboratory. My research examines how trees and crops respond to interacting abiotic stresses, particularly heat, drought, salinity, waterlogging, and their combinations.

My work integrates high-throughput plant phenotyping with whole-plant physiology, stress-biomarker analysis, population genomics, GWAS, and multi-omics. This approach helps connect observable variation in plant performance with genetic, biochemical, and molecular mechanisms of stress tolerance.

I work with Populus species and diverse crop systems, including soybean, wheat, barley, chilli, and tomato. I also validate findings from controlled-environment experiments under field conditions to identify robust traits relevant to climate resilience and sustainable agriculture.

Research Expertise

Understanding plant resilience under interacting environmental stresses.

My research combines controlled-environment and field-based experimentation to understand how plants sense, respond to, recover from, and adapt to environmental stress combinations.

01

Combined Abiotic Stress Biology

Investigating plant responses to heat, drought, salinity, waterlogging, and combinations of these stresses, including growth, survival, recovery, and yield-related traits.

02

High-Throughput Plant Phenotyping

Using non-destructive RGB, chlorophyll fluorescence, thermal, and hyperspectral imaging to quantify plant growth, stress progression, and recovery through time.

03

Whole-Plant Physiology and Biomarkers

Measuring photosynthesis, gas exchange, water relations, chlorophyll fluorescence, oxidative stress, ion balance, phytohormones, and other stress biomarkers.

04

Population Genomics and GWAS

Applying population genomics and genome-wide association studies to link natural genetic variation with stress-response traits, candidate loci, and potential tolerance mechanisms.

05

Multi-Omics Integration

Integrating phenotypic and physiological datasets with transcriptomics, metabolomics, proteomics, genomics, and other molecular data to identify mechanisms of resilience.

06

Field Validation

Evaluating whether findings from controlled experiments remain robust under field conditions, supporting biologically meaningful and agriculturally relevant outcomes.
