About Me

Linking phenotype to mechanism under climate stress.

I am a plant scientist and Postdoctoral Research Associate in Plant Systems Biology at Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL). My current work uses the Advanced Plant Phenotyping Laboratory to characterise heat and drought responses in Populus trichocarpa through RGB, chlorophyll fluorescence, thermal, and hyperspectral (VNIR and SWIR) imaging.

A second strand of my work is method development: designing genome-wide association study (GWAS)-scale experiments, optimising stress assays and imaging workflows, and integrating phenotypic data with omics layers so that results are reproducible and biologically interpretable.

I completed my Ph.D. in Plant and Soil Science at Texas Tech University, where I studied drought, heat, salinity, and waterlogging responses in soybean, cotton, and other crops, including the roles of signalling molecules and phytohormones. Earlier field research in Bangladesh addressed agroforestry and tree–crop interactions. I retain an affiliation with Gazipur Agricultural University, Bangladesh.

Research Expertise

Research areas

Current focus at ORNL

Heat and drought physiology of Populus trichocarpa, studied through non-destructive phenotyping and population-scale experimental design.

01

High-Throughput Phenotyping

Image-based quantification of plant status using RGB, chlorophyll fluorescence, thermal, and VNIR and SWIR hyperspectral imaging to track stress onset, severity, and recovery non-destructively.

02

Heat and Drought Physiology in Populus

Whole-plant and leaf-level responses of P. trichocarpa to heat, drought, and their combination, with attention to genotypic variation in tolerance and recovery.

03

Experimental Design for Genetic Mapping

Planning replicated GWAS-scale stress experiments across many genotypes, including batching, randomisation, and controls that protect data quality.

04

Phenotyping Method Optimisation

Developing and benchmarking stress assays and scoring workflows, and testing how assay duration, imaging modality, and scoring approach affect the signal that can be recovered.

05

Multi-Omics Integration

Relating phenotypic variation to transcriptomic, metabolomic, and genomic layers to identify candidate mechanisms of stress tolerance.

06

Photosynthetic Resilience

Gas exchange, chlorophyll fluorescence, and oxidative stress measurements as functional readouts that anchor imaging-based traits to plant physiology.

Earlier and continuing work

Crop stress physiology and signalling in soybean, cotton, sorghum, and other species; plant–microbe interactions; abiotic stress memory; agroforestry and tree–crop systems; and synthesis reviews on stress tolerance and genome editing.

Scientific Insights

Questions guiding my research

My research connects observable plant traits with physiological and molecular processes to improve understanding of climate-stress tolerance in trees and crops.

Insight 01

Stress responses are dynamic

Plant tolerance cannot be understood only from a single end-point measurement. Repeated imaging and physiological observations make it possible to evaluate stress onset, progression, and post-stress recovery.

Insight 02

Phenotyping needs physiological interpretation

Imaging provides scalable, non-destructive measurements, while gas exchange, chlorophyll fluorescence, and oxidative-stress assays help interpret those patterns in biological terms.

Insight 03

Genotypic variation is a resource

Screening diverse populations under well-designed stress environments can identify variation in tolerance and recovery, supporting genetic mapping and candidate-mechanism discovery.

Insight 04

Combined stresses require integrated approaches

Drought, heat, salinity, and waterlogging can interact in ways that cannot always be predicted from individual stress treatments. Integrated experiments are needed to resolve these responses.

Insight 05

Multi-omics connects trait and mechanism

Integrating phenotyping with transcriptomic, metabolomic, and genomic information helps connect visible stress responses to molecular pathways and candidate genes.

Insight 06

Reproducible design strengthens discovery

Replication, randomisation, environmental controls, and robust data workflows are essential for producing interpretable results from complex phenotyping and genetic experiments.
