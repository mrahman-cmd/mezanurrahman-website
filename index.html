About Me

Plant scientist working toward resilient agricultural systems.

I am Dr. Md. Mezanur Rahman, a plant scientist with approximately 10 years of research experience in plant physiology, biochemistry, molecular biology, agroforestry, and abiotic stress tolerance. I currently work as a Postdoctoral Research Associate in the Plant Systems Biology Group at Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL).

My work combines greenhouse, field, laboratory, and multi-omics approaches to understand how crops and tree species respond to drought, heat, salinity, nutrient deficiency, waterlogging, heavy metals, and other environmental stresses.

I have contributed to interdisciplinary research projects involving crop stress physiology, plant–microbe interactions, signaling molecules, tree–crop interactions, photosynthesis, and climate-smart agriculture.

Research Expertise

Research areas

My research integrates physiological, biochemical, molecular, and omics-based approaches to identify mechanisms that improve plant growth, productivity, and resilience under environmental stress.

01

Abiotic Stress Tolerance

Investigating drought, heat, salinity, nutrient deficiency, waterlogging, submergence, heavy metals, and combined stresses in soybean, maize, sorghum, wheat, rice, cotton, trees, and horticultural crops.

02

Plant–Microbe Interactions

Studying the functions of beneficial microbes, including Bacillus species and Rhizobium japonicum, in root architecture, nodulation, stress adaptation, and crop productivity.

03

Photosynthesis & Multi-Omics

Exploring photosynthetic efficiency, gas exchange, chlorophyll fluorescence, oxidative stress, transcriptomics, metabolomics, hormonomics, and molecular mechanisms under stress.

04

Signaling Molecules & Hormones

Examining the functions of strigolactones, karrikins, abscisic acid, salicylic acid, jasmonic acid, hydrogen sulfide, melatonin, silicon, glutathione, acetic acid, ethanol, and nanoparticles.

05

Tree–Crop Interactions

Conducting agroforestry research on tree–crop interactions involving guava, jackfruit, jujube, citrus, eucalyptus, acacia, and high-value agricultural crops.

06

Climate-Smart Agriculture

Developing environmentally sustainable strategies to improve crop resilience, yield stability, land-use efficiency, and agricultural adaptation to climate change.
