Postdoctoral Research Associate · Plant Systems Biology

How do trees and crops withstand combined abiotic stresses?

Plant Systems Biology · Biosciences Division · Oak Ridge National Laboratory · Tennessee, USA

I investigate how trees and crops respond to interacting abiotic stresses, including heat, drought, salinity, waterlogging, and their combinations. My research integrates high-throughput imaging, plant physiology, stress biomarkers, population genomics, GWAS, and multi-omics to identify resilience traits and mechanisms.

At Oak Ridge National Laboratory I work in Plant Systems Biology and use the Advanced Plant Phenotyping Laboratory for high-throughput phenotyping.

About Me

Linking phenotype, genetics, and molecular mechanisms under combined stress.

I am a plant scientist and Postdoctoral Research Associate in Plant Systems Biology, Biosciences Division, at Oak Ridge National Laboratory. My research investigates how trees and crops respond to combined abiotic stresses, including heat, drought, salinity, waterlogging, and their combinations.

I combine imaging-based phenotypes with physiological, genetic, and molecular data, so that visible traits can be interpreted biologically rather than only measured.

My current work centres on Populus. My crop research spans soybean, cotton, sorghum, maize, wheat, barley, rice, and chilli. Across both, I aim to identify biologically meaningful and agriculturally useful resilience traits.

Research Expertise

Research areas

My research examines plant performance under multiple interacting environmental stresses and links visible phenotypic traits with physiological, genetic, and molecular mechanisms.

  1. ImageRGB, chlorophyll fluorescence, thermal, and VNIR/SWIR imaging
  2. MeasureGas exchange, water status, stress biomarkers, ions
  3. AssociateClimate of origin, population genomics, and GWAS
  4. ExplainTranscriptomics, metabolomics, proteomics
  5. ValidateGreenhouse and field trials

Conceptual workflow for the phenotyping-to-mechanism work; the research areas below give the detail.

01

Combined Abiotic Stress Physiology

Plant responses to drought, heat, salinity, waterlogging, and their combinations, including growth, photosynthesis, water relations, oxidative stress, and recovery.

02

High-Throughput Plant Phenotyping

Non-destructive plant characterisation using RGB, chlorophyll fluorescence, thermal, and hyperspectral imaging across VNIR and SWIR wavelengths.

03

Stress Biomarkers and Physiology

Gas exchange, chlorophyll fluorescence, plant water status, oxidative-stress indicators, hormone responses, and ion balance.

04

Population Genomics and GWAS

Population genetics and genome-wide association studies to connect natural genetic variation with stress-response traits and candidate genes, including associations between genotype tolerance and climate of origin.

05

Multi-Omics Integration

Integration of transcriptomics, metabolomics, proteomics, genomics, physiology, and phenomics to understand resilience mechanisms under stress.

06

Field Validation

Validation of important traits and stress-response mechanisms under field conditions, to improve agricultural relevance and climate resilience.

07

Signalling Molecules and Stress Priming

Roles of phytohormones and signalling molecules, including salicylic and jasmonic acids, strigolactones, melatonin, glutathione, acetic acid, and ethanol, in tolerance to drought, heat, salinity, waterlogging, and metal toxicity.

08

Plant–Microbe Interactions

Effects of Bacillus spp. and Bradyrhizobium japonicum on soybean root architecture and nodulation under drought and heat, and the role of the microbiome in stress adaptation.

Scientific Insights

Questions guiding my research

Insight 01

Combined stresses are not simply additive

Plant responses to drought plus heat, salinity plus waterlogging, or other combinations cannot always be predicted from individual stress responses.

Mittler R. (2006) Trends in Plant Science 11: 15–19. doi:10.1016/j.tplants.2005.11.002

Insight 02

Stress responses are dynamic

Repeated phenotyping and physiological measurement make it possible to distinguish stress onset, acclimation, injury, recovery, and resilience over time.

Insight 03

Phenotyping requires biological interpretation

Imaging provides scalable trait data, while physiological and biochemical measurements show what those traits mean biologically.
