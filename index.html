How do trees and crops withstand combined environmental stresses?

Oak Ridge National Laboratory · Advanced Plant Phenotyping Laboratory · Tennessee, USA

I investigate how trees and crops respond to interacting abiotic stresses, including heat, drought, salinity, waterlogging, and their combinations. By integrating high-throughput imaging, whole-plant physiology, stress-biomarker analysis, population genomics (PopGen), genome-wide association studies (GWAS), and multi-omics—including transcriptomics, metabolomics, and proteomics—I identify stress-response traits and mechanisms that support climate resilience. My research includes Populus species and crop systems such as soybean, wheat, barley, chilli, and tomato. Findings from controlled-environment studies are further validated under field conditions to support biologically meaningful and agriculturally relevant outcomes.

About Me

Linking phenotype, genetics, and molecular mechanisms under combined stress.

I am a plant scientist and Postdoctoral Research Associate in Plant Systems Biology at Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL). My research investigates the responses of trees and crops to combined abiotic stresses, including heat, drought, salinity, waterlogging, and stress combinations that are increasingly important under climate change.

At ORNL, I use the Advanced Plant Phenotyping Laboratory to study Populus species through RGB, chlorophyll fluorescence, thermal, and hyperspectral imaging across VNIR and SWIR regions. I integrate non-destructive phenotyping with whole-plant physiology, stress biomarkers, population genomics, GWAS, and multi-omics approaches, including transcriptomics, metabolomics, and proteomics.

My broader research includes soybean, wheat, barley, chilli, tomato, cotton, sorghum, and other plant systems. I combine controlled-environment experiments with field validation to identify robust stress-response traits, candidate mechanisms, and climate-resilient strategies relevant to both basic biology and agriculture.

I completed my Ph.D. in Plant and Soil Science at Texas Tech University, where I studied crop responses to drought, heat, salinity, waterlogging, and combined stresses, including the roles of signalling molecules and phytohormones. Earlier field research in Bangladesh addressed agroforestry, tree–crop interactions, and environmental resilience.

Research Expertise

Research areas

Combined stress biology in trees and crops

My research examines how plants respond to interacting stresses, rather than treating drought, heat, salinity, or waterlogging as isolated conditions. I investigate stress responses in Populus species and crop systems, with emphasis on traits that can be measured, validated, and translated across controlled and field environments.

01

Combined Abiotic Stress Physiology

Physiological and biochemical responses to drought, heat, salinity, waterlogging, and their combinations in trees and crops, including effects on growth, photosynthesis, water relations, and recovery.

02

High-Throughput Plant Phenotyping

Non-destructive characterisation of plant performance using RGB, chlorophyll fluorescence, thermal, and hyperspectral imaging across VNIR and SWIR wavelengths.

03

Stress Biomarkers and Plant Physiology

Integration of whole-plant physiology, gas exchange, chlorophyll fluorescence, oxidative-stress indicators, hormonal regulation, ion balance, and other stress biomarkers.

04

Population Genomics and GWAS

Population genomics (PopGen), genotype screening, and genome-wide association studies to connect natural variation in stress responses with genetic loci and candidate genes.

05

Multi-Omics Integration

Combining phenotypic and physiological data with transcriptomics, metabolomics, proteomics, genomics, and other omics layers to identify molecular mechanisms of resilience.

06

Field Validation and Translation

Validating key findings from controlled-environment research under field conditions to identify robust, agriculturally relevant traits and stress-tolerance strategies.

Scientific Insights

Questions guiding my research

My research connects visible plant traits with physiology, stress biomarkers, genetics, and molecular mechanisms to improve understanding of climate-stress resilience.

Insight 01

Combined stresses are not simply additive

Heat, drought, salinity, and waterlogging can interact in complex ways. Plant responses to combined stress cannot always be predicted from responses to each stress individually.

Insight 02

Stress responses are dynamic

Repeated imaging, physiology, and biomarker measurements allow stress onset, progression, acclimation, and recovery to be evaluated rather than relying only on single end-point traits.

Insight 03

Phenotyping requires biological interpretation

Imaging produces scalable plant traits, while gas exchange, fluorescence, biochemical markers, and other physiological measurements help explain what those traits mean biologically.

Insight 04

Natural variation enables discovery

Diverse Populus and crop populations offer valuable variation in stress tolerance. PopGen and GWAS help connect this variation to candidate genomic regions and genes.

Insight 05

Multi-omics connects phenotype to mechanism

Transcriptomics, metabolomics, proteomics, and genomics can reveal pathways associated with phenotypic differences in tolerance, susceptibility, and stress recovery.

Insight 06

Field validation is essential

Controlled experiments provide precision, but field validation is essential for determining whether stress-response traits are stable, reproducible, and relevant to agriculture.
